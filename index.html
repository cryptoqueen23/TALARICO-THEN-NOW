WAYBACK + LIVE SITE

Follow the timeline.

Archived versions of jamestalarico.com preserve a detailed policy platform from 2024 and early 2025. By August 2025, that detailed Issues section was no longer displayed on the recovered homepage. In September 2025 the site relaunched for the U.S. Senate campaign. By 2026, a detailed Issues section had returned with a statewide and federal policy agenda.

01

March 4, 2024

2024 PLATFORM

The archived state-representative campaign site openly used the label “Bold, Progressive Legislation” and carried a long issue-by-issue platform.

Health Care

Called health care a “human right,” backed Medicaid expansion and a Medicaid buy-in/public option, and described guaranteed health care as the eventual goal.

Abortion

Supported restoring Roe-era protections, protecting Planned Parenthood, expanding family planning, and putting abortion rights before Texas voters.

Gun Policy

Supported universal background checks and banning the sale of assault weapons.

Immigration

Supported DREAMers, ending indefinite detention, repealing SB 4, opposed requiring undocumented youth to pay out-of-state tuition, and opposed state funding for a border wall.

Marijuana

Supported full legalization, expungement of past marijuana convictions, and using resulting revenue for early-childhood education.

Labor

Supported collective bargaining and repeal of Texas right-to-work laws, which the campaign described as “right-to-work-for-less.”

Climate

Supported greenhouse-gas reduction targets, efficiency standards, and teaching climate change in schools.

Criminal Justice

Supported bail reform, reducing incarceration, restorative justice, marijuana legalization, and divestment from privately owned prisons.

Education

Promoted scientifically accurate sex education, social-emotional learning, restorative justice, and universal full-day early childhood education.

Social Issues

Identified as an LGBTQ+ ally, opposed Republican legislation concerning transgender minors, and opposed mandatory Ten Commandments displays in classrooms.

Voting

Said he led Democratic legislators in leaving Texas to break quorum and backed online voter registration, expanded early voting, and more polling locations.

Housing & Transit

Said “Housing is a human right” and supported affordable-housing efforts, high-speed rail, and expanded public transportation.

August → September 2025

2025 CHANGES

Earlier navigation

DONATE · ACCOMPLISHMENTS · ISSUES · ENDORSEMENTS

August 7, 2025

DONATE · ACCOMPLISHMENTS · ENDORSEMENTS

The detailed Issues platform was no longer displayed on the August 7 homepage recovered from the Wayback Machine. The same page still retained “Bold, Progressive Legislation” and references to marijuana legalization, climate legislation, repealing anti-union laws, transgender minors, and a “Christian Nationalist agenda.”

SEPTEMBER 9, 2025

“It’s time to Start Flipping Tables.”

The new Senate homepage emphasized his Texas roots, teaching background, Presbyterian seminary studies, opposition to billionaire mega-donors and corruption, and a promise to win power back for working people.

Evidence rule: a website change documents a change in presentation. It does not, by itself, prove a policy reversal or explain the campaign’s motive.

Reviewed August 15, 2026

2026 CAMPAIGN

The current Senate website again has a dedicated Issues section. Its categories include Corruption & Democracy, Taxes & Cost of Living, Public Education, Health Care, Freedom, Family & Faith, Immigration & Border Security, Technology, Public Safety & Justice, Labor & Business, Rural Investment, Foreign Policy, and Energy & Environment.
